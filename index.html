Local Data Insights (Romania–Moldova)

Structured data about local markets, including both market structure and the observable digital presence of businesses, for analysis, client discovery, and market entry decisions.

What Local Data Insights is

Local Data Insights (LDI) transforms publicly available digital information about local businesses into structured datasets.

The data is collected, cleaned, and normalized to create datasets that reflect not only the composition of the market, but also the level of observable digital presence of businesses.

What LDI makes visible

LDI is not just a list of companies. It creates a structured digital layer of the market.

This makes it possible to understand:

  • where businesses are located
  • how the market is distributed
  • where competition is concentrated
  • which businesses have limited digital presence
  • where digital gaps and client opportunities exist

What LDI provides

LDI helps move from manual search to working with structured data, making it possible to identify opportunities within a market more quickly.

  • understand market structure and local distribution
  • analyze competition and segment density
  • identify businesses without a website or with weak digital presence
  • find potential clients for digital services and B2B sales
  • explore local markets based on data, not assumptions

Who this is useful for

  • digital and marketing agencies
  • B2B service providers
  • suppliers and distributors
  • SaaS products and business tools
  • entrepreneurs and operators
  • investors exploring local markets

What is included in the datasets

  • business name
  • category
  • city and area
  • geographic coordinates (lat / lon)
  • website availability and link
  • phone and email (where available)
  • observable social media presence
  • Google Maps reference

Example of use

When analyzing a local market, LDI makes it possible to quickly understand:

  • how many businesses are visible in the market
  • how they are distributed geographically
  • the density of competition
  • the share of businesses with limited digital presence

This makes it possible not only to study the market, but to understand where services or products can be offered.
